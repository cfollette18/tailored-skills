# Restrained color design

Give color an information role. Respect the user's palette, audience, content, and brand assets; do not prescribe a universal neutral, accent, or fashionable color combination. Distinctiveness comes from coherent decisions, not from avoiding every familiar color.

## Establish a direction

Choose a quiet base for reading surfaces, a legible ink for text and structure, and an accent with an identifiable job. Decide where attention should go before spreading the accent across components. Explain the role of each color in plain language. Exact color proportions are an outcome of the interface, not a mandatory formula.

Test a representative screen with real content: navigation, a primary action, a dense reading area, and a status message. A mood board or hero section does not reveal whether the direction supports daily work. Use the reference as inspiration while retaining the product's own name, wordmark, typography, and icon vocabulary.

## Avoid generic decoration

Do not give every card a different hue, every icon a colored tile, or every background a glow. Gradients and saturated surfaces need a product-specific purpose rather than acting as default polish. Preserve useful hierarchy; removing all color can make a product harder to use.

Keep brand emphasis distinct from success, warning, error, and destructive actions. A brand red does not mean every red control is dangerous; use labels, icons, placement, and clear feedback. If charts need multiple colors, select accessible series treatments and redundant labels or patterns instead of forcing them into an unsuitable two-color scheme.

Prefer readable secondary text over fashionable low-contrast gray. Use accent variants when the exact brand swatch is unsuitable for small text or button labels. Provider logos and prescribed sign-in controls can remain exceptions without making the product inconsistent.

## Carry the decision into production

Translate the direction into semantic color tokens with explicit interaction states. Review the complete set of screens, dialogs, empty states, and narrow layouts. Remove leftover old-theme tints deliberately, then verify contrast and keyboard focus. Do not change unrelated navigation, content, or product behavior to sell a palette change.
